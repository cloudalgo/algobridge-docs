    Conflicts¶

    A conflict happens when the same record changes in Salesforce and in PostgreSQL before either change has reached the other side. This page explains exactly when AlgoBridge treats a change as a conflict, and the three policies you can choose for resolving one.

    Conflicts only arise on read-write mappings. A read-only mapping never writes to Salesforce, so its table simply follows Salesforce.

    When is it a conflict?¶

    "Both sides changed" has a narrow and exact meaning in AlgoBridge. A conflict exists only while the change log holds an unsent PostgreSQL change for the record, that is, a change still in the NEW or CLAIMED state, at the moment a Salesforce change for the same record arrives.

    Anything else is not a conflict:

    • A PostgreSQL change that has already been sent and accepted is settled. A later Salesforce change is simply applied.
    • A Salesforce change that has already been applied is settled. A later PostgreSQL change is simply sent.

    AlgoBridge makes the decision inside the inbound apply transaction, after it has locked the affected rows. An edit you commit while the batch is waiting for its locks is still seen. An edit that comes after waits for the batch to finish. So there is no window in which a change slips past the check.

    The three policies¶

    You set the policy per mapping in the mapping editor. A read-write mapping must have one; the editor defaults to sf_wins.

    Policy What the incoming Salesforce change does What the pending PostgreSQL change does
    sf_wins (default) Applied to your row. Still sent afterwards.
    pg_wins Skipped this pass. Your row is left exactly as it is. Sent as normal.
    latest_wins Applied if Salesforce's change is the same age or newer; skipped if it is older. Sent as normal.

    The policy only decides what happens to the incoming Salesforce change. It never cancels a pending PostgreSQL change.

    sf_wins¶

    Salesforce's version of the record is written to your table at once. Your pending change is still sent afterwards. Updates send only the fields you changed, so Salesforce ends up holding your values for those fields, and its own values everywhere else. The next poll brings that combined record back to PostgreSQL.

    Choose this when PostgreSQL should always reflect Salesforce as soon as possible, and your own edits are expected to go through.

    pg_wins¶

    The incoming Salesforce change is skipped for this pass, and your row keeps your edit. The watermark still moves past the record, so AlgoBridge does not read it again and again. Your change is then sent. That send gives the record a new SystemModstamp, so the next poll fetches it and brings back the combined record.

    Choose this when PostgreSQL is the system where edits matter most, and you never want an incoming change to overwrite a row with a send still pending.

    latest_wins¶

    AlgoBridge compares two timestamps:

    • the record's SystemModstamp in Salesforce, and
    • the time your newest unsent change to the record was captured in PostgreSQL.

    If the Salesforce change is newer, it is applied as with sf_wins. If your change is newer, the Salesforce change is skipped as with pg_wins. Ties go to Salesforce. Salesforce timestamps are accurate only to the second, and re-applying a record costs nothing, while dropping one could lose an edit.

    Clock accuracy

    latest_wins compares a timestamp taken by Salesforce with one taken by your database, so it is only as good as your database server's clock. sf_wins and pg_wins do not depend on clocks at all.

    Loads and the sweep always side with Salesforce¶

    The conflict policy applies to day-to-day polling. Two jobs follow fixed rules instead:

    • Reload empties the table and loads it again from Salesforce.
    • The daily reconciliation sweep overwrites rows that differ from Salesforce with Salesforce's values. It skips any row that still has a change waiting to be sent. That pending change then goes out and settles the record in the normal way.

    See Salesforce to PostgreSQL for both.

    Why a policy matters¶

    Many sync tools have no conflict setting and simply call their sync eventually consistent, so which edit survives is left to timing. AlgoBridge offers the three policies above per mapping, and the default, sf_wins, gives a predictable result for most installs.

    See Mappings to set a mapping's policy.
